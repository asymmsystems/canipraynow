# Prayer-time method fact-check — SE Asia (Indonesia / Malaysia) & North America (USA / Canada)

Sources: aladhan.com/calculation-methods, api.aladhan.com/v1/methods, praytimes.org/calculation,
kemenag.go.id, JAKIM e-Solat.

## Indonesia — Kemenag — YES

Kemenag officially uses Subuh (Fajr) −20° and Isya (Isha) −18°, defended on kemenag.go.id as
correct per fiqh and field observation of Fajar Shadiq at −19° to −20°. The historical debate ran
the OTHER way (critics wanted a lower angle ~18°); Kemenag retained 20°. 20/18 current and correct.
Ref: kemenag.go.id/en/nasional/kriteria-waktu-subuh-20-derajat-benar-secara-fikih-dan-sains.

## Malaysia — JAKIM — YES

JAKIM (e-Solat) uses Subuh 20° / Isyak 18°. aladhan "Malaysia"/JAKIM method = Fajr 20° / Isha 18°.
Not 18/18, not a minutes-after-sunset scheme. Same angles as Indonesia and Singapore. Malaysia = 20/18.
(The app's authority label saying "MUIS (Singapore)" is the mislabel — authority is JAKIM.)

## USA — ISNA — YES (note)

ISNA method = Fajr 15° / Isha 15° (praytimes.org, aladhan). Widely criticized as too shallow;
many mosques use MWL 18/17. Correct for the "ISNA" label.

## Canada — ISNA — YES / oversimplified

No single national Canadian authority; ISNA (Canada) is the most-used preset, so 15/15 is a
reasonable default. Practice is fragmented (MWL/ISNA/UAQ) and high-latitude cities need adjustments
no single angle captures. Defensible default, not an official national standard.

## Bottom line

All four presets accurate. Indonesia and Malaysia genuinely share Fajr 20° / Isha 18°. Soft spots
are editorial: ISNA 15/15 is a criticized method, and Canada has no single authority.
