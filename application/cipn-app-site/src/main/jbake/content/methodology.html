Rulings & evidence

Every ruling on this page is grounded in the Qurʾān and authentic Sunnah, with each source cited and linked below so you can verify it yourself. For any decision that matters to you, confirm it with a scholar you trust.

New to the terms, or wondering where your prayer times come from? How prayer times are calculated explains twilight angles, your local authority, and every country's method in plain language, with diagrams.

What this tool answers

Standard prayer apps answer when to pray. This tool answers the inverse: whether the current moment falls inside one of the classically defined prohibited or disliked times for prayer (awqāt al-nahy) - and it conditions the answer on what you want to pray, because the ruling genuinely differs by that.

Ruling basis

The default is evidence-based: the ruling derived directly from the Qurʾān and Sunnah, issued as one ruling for everyone rather than by school. On the prohibited times this is the position of the ahl al-hadith - anchored to Ibn Taymiyyah, Ibn Bāz, Ibn ʿUthaymīn, and al-Albānī (via IslamQA) - and it happens to coincide with the Shāfiʿī outcome on almost every cell. The core rule is simple: a prayer with a cause is allowed in every window (a due obligatory prayer, a missed prayer being made up, or any voluntary prayer with a reason such as the mosque greeting); only voluntary prayer with no cause is withheld, and the funeral prayer is best delayed a few minutes past the three sun-times unless there is need.

Two schools ship as explicit alternatives for those who follow them, and choosing one also sets the Asr time: Ḥanafī (which additionally withholds making-up and causal prayers in the strict times, and uses the later Asr) and Shāfiʿī. Mālikī and Ḥanbalī matrices are not yet shipped; the evidence-based default serves everyone in the meantime.

The evidence

The evidence-based ruleset rests on the Qurʾān and these authentic hadith. Every reference below was verified against its published text; the ruleset data carries the same citations on each cell.

  • Prayer is at fixed times. Indeed, prayer has been decreed upon the believers a decree of specified times. — Qurʾān, an-Nisāʾ 4:103.
  • No voluntary prayer after Fajr or Asr. There is no prayer after the morning prayer till the sun rises, and no prayer after the ʿAsr prayer till the sun sets. — Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī; Bukhārī 586, Muslim 827.
  • The three sun-times (prayer and burial withheld). Three times at which the Messenger of Allah forbade us to pray, or to bury our dead: when the sun begins to rise…, when it stands at its height at midday…, and when it draws near to setting. — ʿUqbah ibn ʿĀmir; Muslim 831.
  • A missed prayer is prayed when remembered. Whoever forgets a prayer should pray it when he remembers it; there is no expiation except to pray the same. — Anas ibn Mālik; Bukhārī 597, Muslim 684.
  • A due obligatory prayer is never barred. Whoever catches one rakʿah of the ʿAsr prayer before sunset has caught the ʿAsr prayer. — Abū Hurayrah; Bukhārī 579, Muslim 608.
  • A prayer with a cause, after Asr. The Prophet prayed two rakʿahs after ʿAsr — the two of Zuhr he had missed. — Umm Salamah; Bukhārī 1233, Muslim 834.
  • In the Makkan Ḥaram, any hour. O Banū ʿAbd Manāf, do not prevent anyone from circumambulating this House and praying at any hour he wishes, night or day. — Jubayr ibn Muṭʿim; Abū Dāwūd 1894 (Ṣaḥīḥ, al-Albānī), Tirmidhī 868.
  • The Friday mosque greeting. A man entered during the Friday sermon; the Prophet asked whether he had prayed and, when he said no, told him to stand and pray two rakʿahs. — Jābir ibn ʿAbdillāh; Bukhārī 931, Muslim 875.

In the Shāfiʿī school, prayers with a cause (qaḍāʾ, janāzah, taḥiyyat al-masjid, the sunnah of wuḍūʾ) are valid in the prohibited times; only pure voluntary prayer without a cause is barred. Two exceptions: Friday at the zenith carries no prohibition at all, and within the Makkan Ḥaram no time is prohibited. The Makkah setting is user-controlled and never assumed from GPS.

Mālikī and Ḥanbalī matrices are planned; the data schema already carries slots for them.

Prayer times & astronomy

How the five prayer times are sourced and calculated - your local authority, the calculation methods, and what a twilight angle is - lives on how prayer times are calculated (and the methods in depth). Three points bear directly on the ruling here:

  • The verdict depends only on sunrise, solar zenith, and sunset - astronomical events no calculation convention changes - so it is stable across methods and authorities. Only Fajr and Isha (twilight) vary by method, and they affect the prayer list, not the prohibited-window ruling.
  • The zenith window is derived from astronomical solar noon (the solar transit, NOAA equation of time), never from a timetable - a hard safety rule (below).
  • We compute the times ourselves and are not endorsed by any authority; where your local authority publishes a timetable it is the reference, and we serve its own numbers where we have them (e.g. Sri Lanka's ACJU, matched to within a minute).

Your madhhab setting drives both the Asr time and the rule matrix below, so the default matters (choosing the wrong school moves Asr by about an hour). At high latitudes (roughly above 55° in summer, or the polar circles) where the windows cannot be resolved, the app shows no verdict rather than inventing one - follow local scholarly guidance.

Boundary behaviour

Within ±60 seconds of any boundary the app shows "starting now / ending now" instead of flickering between verdicts. The verdict itself changes exactly at the computed boundary.

Hard safety rule

A wrong "NO" that stops someone from praying an obligatory prayer is the worst failure this product can have. The engine therefore enforces, as an invariant: when you select "Today's fard" while that prayer's time is running, the answer is never a bare prohibition - it is either permitted, permitted-with-warning (the sunset Asr case), or the Fajr-expired guidance to pray qaḍāʾ after sunrise.

Verification & versioning

Rule sets are versioned data files, not code (evidence-0.1.0, hanafi-0.1.0, shafii-0.1.0): each cell and each boundary parameter carries its basis and its citation. When a named scholar reviews the matrix, this page will also carry their name, the review date, and the re-review cadence.
